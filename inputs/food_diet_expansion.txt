When planning meals around dietary restrictions, allergies or chronic conditions, it helps to know exactly which everyday foods fit which diet. The notes below summarise the suitability of twenty common ingredients across the most widely recognised dietary patterns, including vegan, vegetarian, ovolactovegetarian, pescatarian and omnivore diets, gluten free and lactose free diets, diabetic and reflux diets, and the halal, kosher and hindu religious diets.

Whole grain wheat bread is a staple of the bread and bakery category. Made from wholemeal wheat flour, water, yeast and salt, it is suitable for vegan, vegetarian and ovolactovegetarian diets, as well as for pescatarian and omnivore diets. It also fits halal, kosher and hindu diets. Because wheat is one of its main ingredients, whole grain wheat bread is not suitable for a gluten free diet and must be avoided by people with gluten allergy. It contains no dairy, so it is compatible with a lactose free diet.

Brown rice, boiled, sits in the grains and cereals category. It is naturally gluten free and lactose free, and it is widely accepted in vegan, vegetarian, ovolactovegetarian, pescatarian and omnivore diets, as well as in halal, kosher and hindu diets. Because of its lower glycaemic index compared with refined white rice, it is generally considered appropriate for a diabetic diet when consumed in moderate portions.

Quinoa, boiled, is a pseudo-cereal classified within the grains and cereals category. It contains no gluten and no lactose. Quinoa is suitable for vegan, vegetarian, ovolactovegetarian, pescatarian and omnivore diets, for halal, kosher and hindu diets, and for diabetic diets thanks to its balanced carbohydrate and fibre profile.

Red lentils, boiled, belong to the legumes and pulses category. They are naturally gluten free and lactose free. Red lentils are well suited to vegan, vegetarian, ovolactovegetarian, pescatarian and omnivore diets, and they are accepted in halal, kosher and hindu diets. Their high fibre content and low glycaemic index also make them a good choice within a diabetic diet.

Firm tofu, made from coagulated soy milk, falls under the plant based proteins category. It contains no animal product, no gluten and no lactose, and it is therefore suitable for vegan, vegetarian, ovolactovegetarian, pescatarian and omnivore diets, as well as for gluten free, lactose free, halal, kosher and hindu diets.

Raw almonds belong to the nuts and seeds category. They are naturally gluten free and lactose free and contain no animal-derived ingredient. Raw almonds are suitable for vegan, vegetarian, ovolactovegetarian, pescatarian and omnivore diets, and for halal, kosher and hindu diets. They must, however, be strictly avoided by people with a nuts allergy.
